### Description

This publication examines the relationship between digital transformation, innovation territories, and the Healthy Cities movement in Brazil. Through an analytical and forward-looking approach, it explores how the transition from analog to digital public administration can support more inclusive, collaborative, and health-oriented urban development. The article highlights the role of innovation districts and knowledge territories in fostering social integration, civic participation, and evidence-based policymaking, arguing that technological innovation should be closely aligned with public well-being and sustainable urban development.

I was co-author in partnership with MD. Ana Maria Girotti Sperandio, professor at Unicamp.
